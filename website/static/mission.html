Tackling the "Disinformation Paradox"

In an age where disinformation is rampant, and the quality of news sources is political controversy, it is vital to ensure there is an accesible way to validate new information. This "Disinformation Paradox" comes in two parts. First is the time that it takes for experts to validate news. The second is which sources of experts are trustworthy? Oftentimes, the biggest impact of fake news is sourced from the time it took for the news to reach someone who actually could discredit it.

Time-Based Impact of Disinformation

When fake information is initially posted, it has a time window in which there is no response.

Visibility of Responses

Even if experts can disprove the false posts, the likelihood that their response is seen by close to the same number of people is low.

Second, is approaching how we view news as valid. An obvious solution to the timeline problem would be waiting for a news source we trust to verify it, and otherwise assuming that all new information is false. However, that trend causes a centralization of trusted sources, where only a few, large, sources are even believed. Which that phenomonmen leads to a new issue, what if one of those central sources is wrong?

This shows that our approach needs to take a form that is independent from existing media, as relying on them only contributes to that centralization. Due to these restrictions, we felt it was best to try to implement machine learning models, as well as deep learning, to try to get a quick result without having to rely on outside verification. However, that does present a new issue, even if our models have 100% confidence in their predictions, there is no way for it to be completely certain. Therefore, this project should only be used to guide your decision-making on what to believe, and is not an arbiter of truth.
